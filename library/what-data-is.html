PRE-INTERN ACADEMY · DATA FOUNDATIONS

1. What data is and why people use it

Data begins when something about the world is recorded so it can be examined later.

Learning objective

By the end of this book, you should be able to distinguish an event from its record, recognise several forms of data, explain why context matters, and trace recorded evidence into a decision.

A customer places a carton of milk on a checkout counter. The scanner reads a product code. The checkout records a time, a branch, a quantity and a price. The sale happened in the world; the stored facts are data about the sale.

Data is recorded evidence. It can describe an event, object, place, process or measurement. A number can be data, but so can a word, date, image, sound or yes/no answer. What makes it useful is not its shape. It is the connection between the recorded value and what that value is meant to describe.

The event is not the record

A checkout record is not the sale itself. It is a deliberately limited description of the sale. It may record the product and price but not whether the customer was in a hurry. Every record includes some facts and leaves others out.

A useful question

Whenever you meet data, ask: what happened in the world, what was recorded, who recorded it, when was it recorded, and what was left out?

Worked example 1

A checkout receipt says product P-1042, quantity 2, time 18:42. Which part is the event and which parts are data?

  1. Identify what happened: two units of a product were purchased at a checkout.
  2. Identify what was retained: the product identifier, quantity and recorded time.
  3. Keep the two ideas separate. The values describe the event; they are not the physical event.

Answer. The purchase is the event. P-1042, 2 and 18:42 are recorded data about it.

A record can be incomplete or wrong even when the event truly occurred.

Your turn. A temperature sensor stores 6°C at 07:00. Name the event and the recorded data.
answerThe event is the sensor taking a temperature measurement. The recorded data is 6°C together with the time 07:00; the sensor and location are also needed as context.

Data takes many forms

A common beginner mistake is to equate data with numbers. Qubix Superstore uses quantities and prices, but it also uses branch names, product categories, timestamps, delivery photographs and written incident notes. Each form can preserve evidence.

Worked example 2

Classify these as possible data: a price, a branch name, a delivery photograph and a spoken customer comment.

  1. Do not ask whether the item is a number.
  2. Ask whether it records something that can be examined later.
  3. All four retain evidence: an amount, a category, a visual condition and spoken words.

Answer. All four can be data.

Later topics will distinguish structured and unstructured data. This book needs only the broader idea.

Your turn. Are a calendar date and a yes/no inspection result data even though neither is a measurement like height?
answerYes. Each records evidence in a defined form.

A value without context can mislead

Suppose someone sends you the number 14. It could mean fourteen items, fourteen pounds, branch fourteen, fourteen minutes, or a temperature of fourteen degrees. The digits are present, but their meaning is missing.

  • A label says what was recorded.
  • A unit says how a quantity was measured.
  • A time says when it was true.
  • A source says where it came from.
  • A grain says what one complete record describes.
Worked example 3

A message says only “14”. What must you ask before using it in a branch report?

  1. Ask what the value describes.
  2. Ask for its unit if it is a measurement.
  3. Ask which branch or product it belongs to.
  4. Ask when it was observed and which system produced it.

Answer. Request the label, unit, subject, time and source before treating 14 as evidence.

Guessing the missing meaning manufactures data that was never supplied.

Your turn. A table contains the value 6. What two questions would you ask first?
answerWhat does 6 describe, and in what unit or category is it recorded? Time, source and subject usually follow.

Why people record data

People record data because memory is limited and decisions need evidence. A single branch manager can remember that a delivery was late. A company with 48 branches needs consistent records to find repeated delays, compare suppliers and decide where to intervene.

  1. Remember: preserve evidence after an event has passed.
  2. Describe: state what is happening now.
  3. Compare: examine differences across time, products or branches.
  4. Monitor: notice when a process moves outside an expected condition.
  5. Decide: choose an action using the available evidence.
  6. Learn: test whether an action produced the intended result.
Worked example 4

Branch B014 records a stockout for milk at 18:42. How can that one record support a decision?

  1. The record preserves the event after the shelf has been refilled.
  2. Combined with other records, it can reveal how often the product is unavailable.
  3. The branch can compare stockouts with deliveries and sales.
  4. A manager can change an order only after checking that the evidence is accurate and relevant.

Answer. The record becomes useful when it is combined with context and related evidence to support a replenishment decision.

Data supports a decision; it does not make the decision automatically.

Your turn. A supplier arrives late once. What should be recorded before deciding that the supplier is unreliable?
answerRecord the supplier, order, promised time, actual arrival time, branch or hub, and relevant circumstances; then compare with other deliveries rather than judging from an isolated memory.

Data can be wrong, incomplete or harmful

Recorded does not mean true. A scanner can fail, a person can mistype a code, a clock can be wrong and a field can be left blank. Data can also expose people or be collected without a fair purpose. Good data work therefore keeps evidence of uncertainty, protects privacy and documents corrections rather than quietly inventing values.

Do not repair by guessing

If a value is missing, mark it as missing and investigate. Zero is a recorded quantity. Missing means the quantity was not recorded or is not available. They are not interchangeable.

Common mistake: data means numbers that are automatically true

A value looks objective once it appears in a table, so it is easy to treat every number as a fact and every word or image as something else.

Why it is wrong. Numbers, words, dates and images can all be data. Any of them can also be inaccurate, incomplete, badly defined or detached from context. Recording creates evidence, not certainty.
