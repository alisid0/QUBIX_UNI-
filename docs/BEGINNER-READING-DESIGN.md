# Beginner-first reading design

This is the editorial contract for Qubix University reading material. It exists
because simplifying vocabulary alone does not make a lesson beginner-friendly.
The explanation must begin where the learner is, reveal why the idea exists and
only then give the idea its technical name.

## The lesson journey

Every session should answer these questions in this order:

1. **What is happening?** Begin with one concrete, familiar situation.
2. **What problem are we trying to solve?** Give the concept a purpose before a definition.
3. **What is the plain-language idea?** Explain it without relying on earlier technical knowledge.
4. **What do professionals call it?** Introduce the correct term after the learner has a mental model.
5. **Why does the distinction matter?** Show the error, decision or consequence it affects.
6. **Can I recognise it elsewhere?** Transfer the idea beyond the worked example.
7. **Can I use it?** End in a check, exercise and mission that use the same meaning.

## Writing rules

- One main idea per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Define a technical term in the sentence where it first appears.
- Never use a technical word merely to define another technical word.
- Explain an abbreviation before using it.
- Use a shop example when it is natural, not as decoration.
- Preserve distinctions and uncertainty; plain language must not become false language.
- Say what a method cannot tell us as well as what it can.
- Use questions to direct attention, not to manufacture excitement.
- Avoid motivational filler, inflated claims and unexplained career jargon.

## Headings

A heading is a signpost, not a slogan. It should tell a new learner what
situation or question the next paragraph will resolve. Prefer “Why 0 kg and
0 °C do not mean the same thing” to “What zero means,” and “The report says 300.
What are the 300?” to “Ask before you count.” Standard technical terminology
belongs in the explanation once the learner can attach it to something concrete.

## Key terms and the Wiki

- A session opts into no more than four essential key terms.
- Show a compact key-term panel before the main reading.
- Highlight only the first meaningful occurrence of each term in the prose.
- The highlighted term must remain readable as ordinary text and as a keyboard link.
- Each link opens a first-party Qubix Wiki article with a plain definition,
  consequence, worked situation and related concepts.
- Do not link common words, repeat the same link throughout a session or send a
  beginner to a search-results page in place of an explanation.

## Layering, not dumbing down

The first explanation should be understandable without prior study. The second
layer introduces the precise term. The example then shows its boundary, and the
mission makes the learner use it. Precision is retained by ordering the ideas,
not by presenting every qualification in the opening sentence.

## Required alignment

The opening, audio briefing, figure, worked example, focused check and linked
mission must teach the same model. A mission may deepen the reading, but it must
not be the first place where the required concept is explained.
