# Angles and Turns

Status: `AI_DRAFT`, gated

Drafted: 2026-08-11 on founder instruction

## One learning objective

The learner predicts and observes that an angle measures the amount of turn
between two rays, independently of their length, then compares angles with a
right angle.

The board does not yet teach angle sums, intersecting-line theorems, triangle
properties, circle theorems, radians or trigonometric ratios.

## Provisional prerequisites

- recognise a point and a straight line;
- read and compare whole numbers to 180;
- understand a quarter and a half of a whole turn.

These are provisional statements, not approved prerequisite nodes.

## Source record

- Author: George Albert Wentworth
- Title: *Plane Geometry*
- Publisher: Ginn & Company
- Transcription: Project Gutenberg ebook 33063
- Stable record: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33063
- Exact passages: “The Plane Angle,” scans 018–020; “Extension of the Meaning
  of Angles,” scan 021; “Unit of Angles,” scan 022
- Shelf status: `EDITION_REVIEW`

Retained: an angle is an opening between two lines from the same point; its size
depends on the opening rather than side length; rotation generates an angle; a
degree is one three-hundred-and-sixtieth of a revolution.

Modern treatment: the board uses rays, live degree readouts and the current
acute/right/obtuse vocabulary. All board prose, diagrams, controls and exercises
are Qubix-original. The three short exact quotations are stored in
`src/factory/sources.js` with scan-level citations.

## Board structure

1. **An angle is a turn.** Rotate one ray, read the changing degree value and
   visit 30°, 60°, 90°, 120° and 180° benchmarks.
2. **Length does not change the angle.** Hold 60° fixed while arm length changes,
   then compare deliberately misleading long-50° and short-100° drawings.
3. **Use a right angle as a benchmark.** Compare a movable ray with a fixed 90°
   corner, then classify a short sequence as acute, right or obtuse.

Every section has two reading candidates, two interaction candidates and two
checks. The founder selected `S1-B`, both S1 interactions and checks, `S2-B`,
`S2-I1`, both S2 checks, `S3-I1` and both S3 checks on 2026-08-11. S3 still has
no selected reading, so the kept sheet is incomplete.

At founder direction, a 90° state is marked by the perpendicular square rather
than the curved arc used for other angle sizes.

## Misconception to watch

A drawing with longer arms may look larger while representing a smaller angle.
The board changes arm length without changing turn, then puts the misleading
drawings side by side. The degree value is evidence, not merely a label.
