## One learning objective

The learner distinguishes speed, which records how fast, from velocity, which
records both speed and direction.

## Provisional prerequisites

- divide whole numbers;
- read metres, seconds and metres per second;
- recognise left and right as opposite directions.

## Source record

- Authors: Paul Peter Urone and Roger Hinrichs
- Title: *Physics*
- Publisher: OpenStax, Houston, Texas
- Publication date: 26 March 2020
- Passage: §2.2, “Speed and Velocity”
- Stable record: https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/2-2-speed-and-velocity
- Licence: Creative Commons Attribution; attribution to Texas Education Agency
  is required by the source record

Retained: speed is distance divided by time; velocity includes direction and is
displacement divided by time; a complete round trip may have positive average
speed and zero average velocity.

Modern treatment: all prose, numerical examples, controls and graphics are
Qubix-original. No OpenStax exercise or artwork is reused.

## Interaction specification

1. Change distance and time, or compare 2, 4 and 6 m/s over equal times.
2. Reverse a 4 m/s velocity, or compare equal speeds in opposite directions.
3. Run a fixed out-and-back trip, or build a route from 10 m directional steps
   while distance, displacement, average speed and average velocity update.

## Misconception to watch

Velocity is not merely another word for speed. Direction can change while speed
stays fixed. Returning to the start makes displacement and average velocity
zero, but it does not erase distance or average speed.
